1. Purpose & Legal Basis

Wellfare ehf. ("SameFare") is subject to Icelandic Act no. 140/2018 on Measures against Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing (Lög um aðgerðir gegn peningaþvætti og fjármögnun hryðjuverka), as amended, and to the EU Anti-Money Laundering Directives as incorporated into Icelandic law through the EEA Agreement.

This document sets out the internal procedure that SameFare follows to:

  • Identify transactions or account activity that may constitute money laundering or terrorist financing.
  • Escalate concerns internally to the designated compliance officer.
  • Decide whether to file a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) with the relevant Icelandic authority.
  • Maintain the required records of all reviews and filings.

Compliance with this procedure is mandatory for all personnel of Wellfare ehf. who have access to platform data, payment records, or user accounts.

2. Designated Compliance Officer (MLRO)

Wellfare ehf. designates a Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO) who is the sole person authorised to make the decision to file — or not file — a SAR with the relevant authority.

Deputy MLRO (if MLRO is unavailable) Ástþór Arnórsson — Director, Wellfare ehf. samefare@samefare.com

The MLRO is responsible for:

  • Receiving all internal suspicion reports.
  • Evaluating whether a SAR filing is required.
  • Filing SARs with the relevant authority where required.
  • Maintaining the SAR decision log described in section 9.
  • Ensuring this procedure is kept current and is reviewed annually.

In the event the MLRO is personally involved in or connected to a suspicious transaction, the matter must be escalated directly to the Deputy MLRO or, if no deputy is designated, to the company's legal counsel without involving the MLRO.

3. What Triggers a Review

Any of the following should prompt an internal report to the MLRO. The list is indicative, not exhaustive — any genuine suspicion, however it arises, must be escalated.

3.1 Platform-level red flags

  • A single account posting or booking an unusually high number of trips in a short period with no plausible personal travel explanation.
  • Trip prices significantly above or below platform norms for the route — particularly if the same driver–passenger pair repeats.
  • Repeated booking and immediate cancellation cycles with no completed trips from the same account or device.
  • Multiple accounts sharing the same device fingerprint, IP address, or payment method with no obvious household/family explanation.
  • Payments originating from cards issued in jurisdictions on the FATF high-risk or monitored list.
  • A verified driver with a PEP (Politically Exposed Person) flag or adverse media hit identified during KYC screening.

3.2 Verification red flags

  • A user submitting multiple different identity documents before one is accepted.
  • Inconsistency between a user's stated personal details and their identity document.
  • A user who is unusually reluctant to complete identity verification, or who queries why it is required.
  • A KYC screening hit against a sanctions list, PEP database, or adverse media that is not clearly a false positive.

3.3 Payment red flags

  • A cardholder disputing a charge as "not authorised" after completing a verified booking, where the booking record clearly shows their participation.
  • Multiple chargebacks or refunds linked to the same driver's bookings or payout account within a short period.
  • A driver account accumulating disbursements from a high volume of low-value or same-day bookings that do not correspond to plausible routes.

3.4 Behavioural red flags

  • A user contacting support to ask how payment tracking works, how long records are kept, or whether transactions are reported to authorities.
  • A user offering to pay extra to avoid the identity verification requirement.
  • Any communication suggesting that a trip or payment is being arranged to conceal the origin of funds.

4. Internal Reporting — How to Escalate

Any person with access to SameFare systems who observes a red flag listed in section 3, or who has a genuine suspicion for any other reason, must report it to the MLRO as follows:

  1. Send an email to samefare@samefare.com with the subject line: "INTERNAL AML REPORT — [date]".
  2. Include: the user ID(s) and/or booking ID(s) concerned; a factual description of what was observed; the date and time of the observation; any supporting screenshots or data.
  3. Do not discuss the concern with the user, other platform users, or anyone outside the MLRO chain. See section 7 (tipping-off prohibition).
  4. Do not take any action on the account (suspension, payment hold, or otherwise) before speaking with the MLRO unless the situation is urgent, in which case apply a temporary payment hold and notify the MLRO immediately.

The MLRO will acknowledge receipt within 24 hours on business days.

5. Decision-Making Process

On receiving an internal report, the MLRO will:

  1. Review the report — examine the account history, booking records, payment data, and KYC/screening results within 48 hours of receipt (or immediately if a payment is pending).
  2. Classify the suspicion — one of three outcomes:
    • No further action — the concern can be clearly explained by legitimate behaviour. Document the reasoning and close.
    • Enhanced monitoring — the concern is ambiguous. Place the account under enhanced manual monitoring for 30 days. Document the reasoning and review date.
    • SAR required — there are reasonable grounds to suspect money laundering or terrorist financing. Proceed to section 6.
  3. Document the decision in the SAR Decision Log (section 9) regardless of outcome.

The standard for filing is reasonable suspicion — not certainty, not proof. If, after consideration, a reasonable person in the MLRO's position could suspect that the activity relates to money laundering or terrorist financing, a SAR should be filed. Erring on the side of filing is always preferable to erring on the side of not filing.

6. Filing a SAR with the Authorities

6.1 Filing authority

SARs must be filed with the competent Icelandic authority under Act no. 140/2018. Legal counsel should confirm the correct recipient before any live filing. The likely candidates are:

  • Fjármálaeftirlitið (FME) — the Icelandic Financial Supervisory Authority, which supervises obliged entities under the AML Act.
  • Embætti ríkislögreglustjóra (National Police Commissioner) — which operates the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) function in Iceland.

Until legal confirmation is obtained, the MLRO should contact FME directly at fme.is to confirm the correct SAR submission channel prior to the first filing.

6.2 What to include in the SAR

A SAR should contain at minimum:

  • The identity of the subject (name, date of birth, address, and ID document reference from the KYC record).
  • A factual description of the suspicious activity — what was observed, when, and why it gives rise to suspicion.
  • The transaction amounts and dates involved.
  • Any action already taken (e.g. payment hold, account suspension).
  • The date of filing and the name of the person filing.

6.3 Consent to proceed

Where a transaction is pending at the time of filing, the MLRO may seek consent from the authority to proceed with the transaction rather than hold it indefinitely. If consent is not received within 7 working days of filing, the transaction may proceed unless the authority has applied for a restraint order.

6.4 After filing

  • Record the filing in the SAR Decision Log (section 9) immediately.
  • Do not inform the subject that a SAR has been filed — see section 7.
  • Retain all records used to prepare the SAR for a minimum of 5 years from the date of filing.

7. Tipping-Off Prohibition

Under Act no. 140/2018, it is a criminal offence to disclose — directly or indirectly — that a SAR has been filed or that an AML investigation is underway. This is known as the tipping-off prohibition.

Practically, this means:

  • Do not tell the user that their account is under AML review.
  • Do not tell the user that a SAR has been or may be filed.
  • Do not explain to the user why their payment has been held or their account suspended if the reason relates to an AML investigation.
  • If asked why an account is suspended, the approved response is: "Your account is under review. We are not able to provide further information at this time. If you believe this is in error, please contact samefare@samefare.com."
  • Do not discuss the SAR or the investigation with colleagues who do not need to know, or with anyone outside the company.

The tipping-off prohibition applies even after the investigation is closed. A SAR and its contents remain confidential indefinitely.

8. Account & Payment Holds Pending Review

Where an account is under AML review, the MLRO may instruct one or more of the following measures:

  • Payment hold — driver disbursements withheld pending the outcome of the review. The user is notified that their payment is under review without being given AML-related reasons.
  • Booking suspension — the account may not post new trips or make new bookings while the review is open.
  • Account suspension — the account is deactivated pending outcome.

These measures may be applied before or after a SAR is filed. If applied before, they must not themselves constitute tipping-off — the user may be told only that a routine review is taking place.

If the review concludes with no further action, any holds should be released promptly and the account restored, unless there are independent grounds for suspension (e.g. driver accountability thresholds).

9. Record-Keeping

The MLRO must maintain a SAR Decision Log — a private, dated record of every internal AML report received and every decision made. The log must be stored securely and retained for a minimum of 5 years from the date of each entry, in accordance with Act no. 140/2018 Art. 24.

Field What to record
Reference number Sequential internal reference (e.g. SAR-2026-001)
Date received Date the internal report was submitted to the MLRO
Subject(s) User ID(s) and/or booking ID(s) — not full names in the log index
Nature of suspicion Brief factual description of the red flag(s) observed
Decision No action / Enhanced monitoring / SAR filed
Date of decision Date the MLRO reached a conclusion
SAR reference (if filed) Reference number or acknowledgement from the authority
Outcome Any feedback from the authority; any action taken on the account
Retention expiry Date after which this record may be deleted (date of entry + 5 years)

The log may be maintained as a password-protected spreadsheet or secure document. It must not be stored on the same public-facing server as the SameFare application. A printed and signed copy of each SAR filed should be retained alongside the log.

10. Staff Awareness & Training

Every person with access to SameFare's platform data, payment records, or user accounts must:

  • Read this procedure in full before being granted access.
  • Confirm in writing (email to the MLRO is sufficient) that they have read and understood it.
  • Re-confirm annually, or whenever a material update to this procedure is issued.

The MLRO is responsible for ensuring all relevant personnel are aware of changes to this procedure and of any updates to Icelandic AML law that affect it.

11. Policy Review

This procedure will be reviewed by the MLRO:

  • Annually — at minimum, on or before the anniversary of the adoption date.
  • Following any SAR filing — to assess whether the procedure worked as intended.
  • Following any material change to Icelandic AML law, FME guidance, or the structure of the SameFare platform.

Version history must be maintained. Superseded versions must be retained for at least 5 years.
