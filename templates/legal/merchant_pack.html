2. Business Model & Product

2.1 What SameFare is

SameFare is an online rideshare cost-sharing marketplace serving Iceland. Drivers who are making a journey and have empty seats post available rides. Passengers search for and book a seat on a matching route. The driver is reimbursed for a proportional share of their trip costs (fuel, tolls); no profit motive is involved for the driver.

SameFare operates as a marketplace platform and is the merchant of record for bookings. Payments are collected by SameFare through our licensed payment service provider (the PSP); SameFare retains the service fee (18%) and pays each driver their cost contribution (82%) as a separate payout to the driver's Icelandic bank account after the trip. SameFare holds the driver's share only transiently, between capture and payout, and tracks entitlements in an internal payout ledger. Revenue is generated exclusively through the per-booking service fee.

2.3 Market & geography

Iceland only. All transactions are denominated in Icelandic Króna (ISK). Users are required to hold a government-issued identity document from Iceland or the EEA. The platform is available exclusively via the web (samefare.com) — no third-party app stores are used.

2.4 Legal classification

Cost-sharing rideshare is legally distinct from commercial passenger transport (taxi, ride-hailing). Under Icelandic law and EU guidance (including Commission interpretations of Directive 2006/123/EC), genuine cost-sharing platforms — where the driver recoups costs rather than generating profit — do not require a transport operator licence. SameFare's Terms & Conditions explicitly prohibit drivers from setting prices above their actual per-seat cost share.

3. Payment Architecture

All card payments are card-not-present (CNP), processed online. SameFare operates a three-stage payment model to handle the variable gap between booking time and departure time while managing authorisation expiry risk.

3.1 Three-stage model

StageActionTiming
1 — Card validation A zero-value charge (0 ISK) confirms the card is live and SCA-compliant. No funds are held. Card is tokenised in the PSP vault — SameFare stores no raw card data. At checkout (customer-initiated transaction)
2 — Pre-authorisation Full booking amount pre-authorised via a merchant-initiated transaction (MIT). Funds are ring-fenced on the card but not yet captured. Automatically 24 hours before departure. If the booking is made within 24 hours of departure, pre-authorisation fires immediately at checkout as part of the same CIT.
3 — Capture & payout Pre-authorisation captured in full to SameFare automatically at scheduled departure time. SameFare retains the service fee and pays the driver the cost contribution as a separate payout after the trip. No refund on passenger-fault outcomes. If the driver cancels, the pre-auth is voided — no capture is made. Automatically at scheduled departure time

3.2 Authorisation window

Pre-authorisation is deliberately deferred to 24 hours before departure to ensure the authorisation remains valid through to capture. Standard card network authorisation validity is 7 days; a 24-hour window eliminates any risk of expiry regardless of how far in advance the booking was made.

3.3 Driver disbursement

At scheduled departure time, capture fires automatically and the full amount is collected by SameFare as merchant of record. SameFare retains the service fee (18%) and pays the driver their cost contribution (82%) as a separate payout to their Icelandic bank account (via Blikk), normally shortly after the trip completes. SameFare holds the driver's share only transiently, between capture and payout; entitlements and payouts are tracked in an internal payout ledger.

3.4 Pre-authorisation failure & retry

If the T−24h MIT pre-authorisation fails (e.g. insufficient funds, expired card):

  1. Passenger is notified immediately by SMS and email.
  2. A 2-hour window opens for the passenger to update their card via the platform.
  3. The passenger returns to the platform, saves a new card via an SCA-authenticated hosted card page (customer-initiated transaction), and the merchant-initiated authorisation re-fires.
  4. The amount is unchanged from the original booking — there is no retry surcharge. The total is the same one the passenger already consented to.
  5. If the passenger does not update their card within 2 hours, the booking is automatically cancelled, the seat is released, and no charge is made.

Because the retry re-uses the original consented amount with fresh SCA on the new card, no authorisation ever exceeds the passenger's original consent. Chargeback risk is minimal.

3.5 Transaction flow summary

  1. Passenger books a seat → SCA / 3-D Secure challenge issued; card validated with a 0 ISK charge.
  2. Card tokenised in PSP vault; booking confirmed pending pre-authorisation.
  3. T−24h: MIT pre-authorisation for full booking amount placed automatically. (Exception: if departure is ≤24h from booking time, pre-authorisation is folded into the CIT at step 1 — no separate MIT is issued.)
  4. If pre-auth fails: passenger notified; 2-hour window for one CIT retry attempt (same amount, 18% fee — no surcharge). If retry is also declined, or window expires unused: booking cancelled, no charge.
  5. Scheduled departure time reached → capture instruction sent automatically to PSP (no driver action required).
  6. PSP confirms capture → the full amount settles to SameFare; SameFare retains 18% and pays the driver 82% as a separate payout to their Icelandic bank account after the trip.

5. Refund & Cancellation Policy

The cancellation model is binary, governed entirely by whether the T−24h pre-authorisation has been placed. Full terms are published at samefare.com/terms (§8).

5.1 Cancellation outcomes by scenario

ScenarioCapture actionPassenger outcomeDriver outcome
Passenger cancels before T−24h (pre-auth not yet placed) No capture No charge Nothing — seat released
Passenger cancels after T−24h (pre-auth placed) Full capture No refund. Seat reservation = service fulfilled. Receives cost contribution
Passenger no-show Full capture No refund Receives cost contribution
Trip completed Full capture at scheduled departure time No refund Receives cost contribution
Driver cancels (any time) Pre-auth voided — no capture No charge Nothing
Driver no-show (passenger reports within T+4h) Fare was captured to SameFare at departure. On a confirmed report, SameFare refunds the passenger and cancels the driver's payout for that booking. Full platform refund — cost contribution + service fee — to the passenger's card. No chargeback required. Account suspended immediately; the driver's payout for the booking is cancelled/reversed.

5.2 PSP capture mechanics

No partial captures are required. Every PSP action is either a full capture or a void of the pre-authorisation. This eliminates split-capture complexity entirely.

Driver no-show. The fare is captured to SameFare at departure as normal. If a confirmed passenger reports the driver as a no-show within 4 hours of departure, SameFare issues a full platform refund (cost contribution + service fee) to the passenger's card through the PSP and cancels the driver's payout for that booking. Resolution is a platform-level refund, not a card-scheme chargeback. SameFare's platform records (booking confirmation, scheduled departure timestamp, no-show report) are retained as evidence.

Retry transaction amounts. In the pre-auth failure retry scenario, the passenger saves a new card and the authorisation re-fires for the same amount as the original booking (contribution + 18% service fee — no surcharge). The PSP will see two distinct authorisation attempts on the same card: the failed MIT and the subsequent successful authorisation at the same amount. Both are tied to the same booking reference in SameFare's records.

6. Chargeback & Dispute Management

6.1 Chargeback liability model

SameFare is the merchant of record, so any card chargeback is filed against SameFare for the full transaction amount (cost contribution + service fee). SameFare manages this exposure operationally rather than by pushing liability to a sub-merchant: every transaction is SCA-authenticated, the cancellation/no-show policy is explicitly acknowledged at checkout, and driver-side failures are resolved by a direct platform refund — which removes the main reason a passenger would otherwise raise a chargeback. Where the driver is at fault (e.g. a confirmed no-show), SameFare recovers its outlay by cancelling or reversing that driver's payout.

This model concentrates card liability on SameFare but pairs it with strong authentication, clear consent, and a fast in-platform refund path, keeping realised chargeback rates low while aligning the financial consequence of a failure with the party responsible (via payout reversal).

6.2 Risk mitigants

  • Mandatory KYC on both sides — passengers and drivers are identity-verified before any transaction. Anonymous transactions are not possible.
  • 3-D Secure on all CITs — SCA shifts liability to the issuer for authenticated transactions. Pre-auth MIT is covered by the deferred-payment SCA exemption.
  • Booking confirmation emails — passengers receive timestamped booking confirmation, trip details, and driver information at booking and prior to departure.
  • Clear no-refund policy — published in T&C; passengers explicitly acknowledge the policy (including the no-refund clause for post-T−24h cancellations) at checkout via a mandatory checkbox.
  • Automated capture audit trail — capture fires at the scheduled departure time via a time-locked background task, creating a timestamped record. Disputes claiming "services not provided" are countered with the scheduled departure timestamp and booking record.
  • Driver accountability system — drivers are suspended after one confirmed no-show, reducing repeat-offender risk. No-show reports are logged with timestamps for dispute evidence.
  • In-platform messaging — all passenger–driver communication is logged on-platform, available for dispute representment.

6.3 Dispute response process

For any chargeback (SameFare is the merchant of record), SameFare can supply to the acquirer or issuer: booking confirmation records, scheduled departure timestamp, cancellation policy acknowledgement, checkout checkbox confirmation, communication logs, SCA/3-D Secure authentication results, and KYC verification status for both parties. SameFare targets a response time of 3 business days for chargeback representment requests.

Where a chargeback reflects a driver-side failure, SameFare additionally recovers its outlay by cancelling or reversing the driver's payout for the affected booking.

6.4 Expected chargeback rate

SameFare is a launch-stage platform. As merchant of record, SameFare bears card chargeback exposure on the full transaction, but this is mitigated by SCA authentication on every transaction, the explicit policy acknowledgement at checkout, and a direct in-platform refund path for driver-side failures (which removes the main reason a passenger would otherwise chargeback). Transaction and dispute records can be provided to the PSP upon request.

7. User Verification & KYC

Full KYC is a non-negotiable precondition for any transaction on the platform. Users who have not completed verification cannot make or receive payments.

7.1 Verification provider

Identity verification is performed by Didit Technologies, an accredited identity verification provider. All verification flows are hosted on Didit's infrastructure.

7.2 Passenger verification

Before completing a first booking, passengers must submit one of:

  • Icelandic or EEA national ID card
  • Passport (any nationality)
  • Driving licence valid in Iceland (Icelandic or EEA-issued)

Didit performs: document authenticity verification (MRZ, chip reading where available, security feature checks); liveness detection; and biometric face matching against the document photograph. SameFare stores only the verification session reference and outcome — no document images or biometric data are held on SameFare servers.

7.3 Driver verification

Before posting a ride, drivers must submit a driving licence valid in Iceland. A single licence submission satisfies both identity verification and driver eligibility verification simultaneously. Drivers are subject to enhanced due diligence and must self-declare that their vehicle holds valid civil liability insurance covering cost-sharing passengers and a current roadworthiness certificate.

7.4 Re-verification

SameFare may require re-verification at any time — for example, when a submitted document approaches expiry, when unusual account activity is detected, or when regulatory changes require re-screening. Failure to re-verify results in suspension of payment features.

8. AML & Sanctions Compliance

8.1 Regulatory framework

SameFare operates under Icelandic Act no. 140/2018 on Measures against Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing, EU Anti-Money Laundering Directives as transposed into Icelandic law, and regulations issued by Fjármálaeftirlitið (FME).

8.2 Sanctions & PEP screening

All users are automatically screened at onboarding against:

  • UN Security Council consolidated sanctions list
  • EU consolidated financial sanctions list
  • OFAC Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) list
  • Politically Exposed Persons (PEP) databases
  • Adverse media (automated screening for negative news linked to financial crime, fraud, or terrorism)

Screening is performed by Didit Technologies as part of the identity verification flow. Screening hits trigger manual review before an account is activated. Re-screening of existing users is applied where triggered by unusual account activity, updated watchlist alerts, or regulatory requirement.

8.3 Transaction monitoring

Ongoing automated transaction monitoring flags patterns including: unusually high booking volumes per account, prices significantly outside platform norms, repeated booking/cancellation cycles with no completions, multiple accounts sharing device or payment method, and payments from cards issued in high-risk jurisdictions. Flagged accounts are reviewed and may have disbursements withheld pending investigation.

8.4 Suspicious Activity Reporting

SameFare files Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) with the relevant Icelandic authority as required by Act no. 140/2018. Note: the precise competent authority for SAR filing under Icelandic law (FME or the National Police Commissioner / Embætti ríkislögreglustjóra) should be confirmed with Icelandic legal counsel prior to any live SAR submission. Records are retained for a minimum of 5 years in accordance with Act no. 140/2018 Art. 24.

8.5 Full KYC/AML policy

The complete KYC & AML Policy is published at samefare.com/kyc-aml.
